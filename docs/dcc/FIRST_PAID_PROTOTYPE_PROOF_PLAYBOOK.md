# DCC | First Paid Prototype Proof Kit (v0.1)

**Status:** Reusable internal production template. No client publication permission is implied by this file.
**Use:** Document one scoped, paid, real fabrication job from an existing object to a measured first prototype and review.
**Related:** [Image Shot List](./IMAGE_SHOT_LIST.md) · [Fabrication OS](./FABRICATION-OS.md) · [Resin Shot List](../workshops/RESIN_PRINTING_MEDIA_SHOT_LIST.md)

## Editorial principle

**Lead with the human problem, not the machine.** Show what the artist already made; explain why that form does not yet work for its intended use; document the actual intervention; show measurable evidence; let the artist judge whether the prototype succeeds. Do not invent results or describe a speculative image as documentary proof.

Working story sentence: **"The artist had the form. The material made it impractical. Could a lightweight prototype preserve what mattered?"**

DCC's role is to make necessary people, tools and production capacity available; document the useful learning; and coordinate only where that coordination adds value. Credit the originating artist and all real operators.

## Pre-publication gates

- A job may be **scoping**, **quoted**, **approved**, **in production**, **QA/review**, or **completed**. Never imply "paid client," "delivered," "successful," or "finished earrings" without corresponding verified records.
- Record the client's **separate express opt-ins** for public name, source-object imagery, CAD screenshots, process photos, finished-object photos, voice/face, third-party operator credit, and public case study. Default to private.
- A private proposal link or WhatsApp membership is not permission to publish. Media hosted on an accessible CDN is not inherently private, even when shown in a gated page.
- Confirm location permission (studio/venue), operator consent, and artwork ownership/provenance; do not publish unrelated people, email addresses, computer screens, labels or identifiable clients in the background.
- Do not film unsafe resin practices. Use the actual machine, correct PPE, ventilation and a safe controlled wet zone; never stage ungloved handling of uncured resin. Do not encourage wearing prototype material until intended skin-contact use has been assessed.
- Do not imply the DCC network owns a borrowed machine or that a partner has agreed to work before acceptance.
- No price, payment, owner/operator split or proprietary file should be publicly shown without explicit authorization.

## The minimum proof: 1 still, 1 film, 1 record

### Still hero — "The problem in one frame" (4:5, 3000×3750 or larger)

Real physical object on a clean neutral workbench at the actual studio; one hand gives human scale. Make texture legible. Include a small scale or calipers only if they do not distract. Take a clean version **without typography** for reuse. Do **not** imply the brass source is a wearable finished earring.

**Alt/caption draft, for private use:** "The artist's existing metal cast at the studio, photographed before digital capture. Its surface detail is worth preserving; its mass is the problem the prototype will test."

### Video — 35–50-second documentary Reel (9:16; 1080×1920 delivery, capture 4K when possible)

| Seconds | Real shot | On-screen line / narration | Proof required |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–4 | Extreme macro of existing metal piece | "This started as a sculpture." | Actual source object |
| 4–9 | Source resting on digital scale; readout in focus | "But it was too heavy to wear." | Record actual **grams** |
| 9–15 | Caliper/ruler, neutral turntable views | "The challenge: keep the texture. Lose the weight." | True dimensions |
| 15–22 | Actual geometry capture / modeling screen | "We translated the form into a digital study." | Capture method and file version |
| 22–30 | Real slicing, machine, and safe post-process B-roll | "Then tested a lighter material." | Operator, material, run log |
| 30–37 | **Cured** prototype on scale beside metal reference | "Same idea. Different material and mass." | Actual weights; no invented percentage |
| 37–45 | Artist review / careful object handling | "Did it keep what the artist cared about?" | Authentic client assessment |
| 45–50 | Finished comparison still with factual project status | "Prototype 1 / Learning returned" | Actual result, permitted credits |

If there is no prototype yet, stop at 15 seconds: **"The problem / We are beginning the test."** Never fill missing shots with AI-generated documentary footage. Mark conceptual imagery clearly when used.

Use original ambient sound (metal handling, caliper, equipment) and subtitles; license any music or omit it. Do not over-brand. Keep the artist's work central, with DCC credited as coordination/fabrication.

### Before/after image or carousel — only after a physical prototype exists

1. Original physical object / actual artist credit
2. Why it could not serve its intended use (measured weight)
3. Source-to-geometry method (real model, approved screenshot)
4. First cured print, process and operator credit
5. Two objects on the **same scale** or a controlled side-by-side with factual measurements
6. Artist verdict + what is still unresolved; no fake "success"

Aim for one carousel only when the information differs from the Reel.

## Bakehouse first capture session: 30–45 min, no machine required

| Capture ID | Photo/video | How |
|---|---|---|
| 01_source_macro | 4:5 portrait and 5–8-second clip | Neutral matte background, raking side light |
| 02_source_360 | 12–20-second slow video | Rotation across front, back, sides |
| 03_source_weight | Photograph + 5-second video | Zeroed scale, reading in grams; record scale precision |
| 04_source_dims | Three photographs | Length, widest width, maximum depth; calipers in frame |
| 05_attachment | 4:5 macro | Existing loop or attachment location, no imagined findings |
| 06_workspace | 16:9 still | Real work surface / studio; avoid unapproved faces and screens |
| 07_notes | Private record | Condition, texture priorities, physical source custody and next step |

**Do not assume scanning is solved by these pictures.** Photogrammetry of reflective metal may need controlled diffuse light, cross-polarization, a specialist scanner, or an alternative source. Do not coat or modify a client's artwork without permission.

## Fabrication field record — each run

Record: job ID; real quote/approval reference; actual operator and machine owner; machine model and site; model file name/hash/version; dimensions and orientation; resin/filament manufacturer/product/batch; wall thickness and drainage if hollow; slicer/profile; estimated and actual material; setup/machine/operator time; wash and cure recipe; date; failure notes; finished **dry and fully cured** part mass; attachment evaluation; comparison photos; client review and sign-off.

**Separate:** (a) client job / contract, (b) individual machine run, (c) public case-study candidate. An attempt is not a delivered outcome, a friendly learning exchange is not paid work, and a quote is not a payment.

## Preliminary social copy — use only when supported

**Before a print:** "An artist brought us a beautiful physical form with a practical problem: it's too heavy for the jewelry she wants to make. We're exploring a lightweight prototype, starting by documenting the original and its dimensions. No final material or outcome yet."

**After a real first print:** "We made the first physical study. Here's the original weight, prototype weight, material, what detail survived, and what still needs work. Next comes the artist's review."

**After client-approved success:** "A physical idea became a wearable study through modeling, fabrication and testing. The finished result and lessons are shared with the artist's permission. Credits: [actual names and roles]."

Do not announce a "successful paid job" until scope approval, payment evidence, physical handoff and client verdict are confirmed.

## Publishing hierarchy

1. **Private**: raw source, operations, quote, machine settings, unfinished test results.
2. **Client preview**: selected process timeline on the gated proposal page, with verified status.
3. **Moises.Tech**: one artist-centered editorial case study if the client opts in.
4. **DCC Miami**: short fabrication-network proof with operator credits and lessons (separate opt-in).
5. **Social**: one Reel + one concise carousel, not a constant promotion loop.
